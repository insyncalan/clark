**Version 0.2.0 of clark (Beta): the editor, in your hands. Released [date]. Mac only.**

The whole of clark is **Beta** until version 1.0: it works and is tested, but expect rough edges and changes between versions.

This version turns the report into something you edit on the page itself, and adds a milestones timeline, safety copies of your data, and "archive" in place of "delete". Your data stays on your computer.

**What is new**

*Edit the report where you read it*
- **View and Edit.** Switch between them without reloading. In View nothing can be changed by accident. In Edit you can add a text section, rename it, move it up or down, or hide it. Every one of these has Undo, and a hidden section is not in the published file.
- **Change work items in place.** Click a status, priority, owner or due date in the Work items list and change it there, with the mouse or the keyboard. Each change is one step with Undo. You can add an action, risk, issue, decision, milestone or use case from the page.
- **The eye.** The eye on a row hides it from the published page, with Undo.
- **A contents list on the side and a top bar that stays in view** when you scroll a long report.

*Find your way round*
- **Back and Forward work as in any web page.** Every page and project has its own address. The trackpad swipe, the mouse's back and forward buttons and the keyboard all step through them. Filtering and scrolling do not fill up your history, and a reload opens the same page. The published file's tabs step the same way.
- **Work items filters** you can read at a glance: Open, Closed and All with counts, one "Filter by" row, and the active filters shown as tags you can clear in one click.
- **Add buttons** that grow to say what they add. **Import and export** are icons with a tooltip. A **profile circle** with your initials holds Settings and **Dark mode**, which now covers the whole editor.

*Milestones and the dashboard*
- **Milestones timeline and calendar**, with a switch between them and a legend underneath. Zoom the timeline, see five milestones in the side list and open the rest with Show all. A milestone that slipped shows a marker from its agreed date to its new date, with the number of days.
- **Movement:** every change of a milestone date, in order, in plain words.
- **At a glance tiles** for health, overdue actions, high risks and issues, and decisions needed. The health tile has a button you click to set the health and a reason you edit in place. Tiles can be moved and hidden.
- **Use cases summary:** a strip of counts by status, a slim row for each use case, and its standing and linked work items opening in place.

*Publishing*
- **Publish to client** is a separate, deliberate choice, ticked each time. Before the file goes into the client folder, clark checks the finished file: hidden rows, rows not marked for the client, Sensitive rows and internal notes are all absent. If a check fails, nothing is copied and the message says why.
- Client output and team output are kept apart.
- **The Publish dialog lists what is incomplete or stale:** work items with no owner or no date, and items not reviewed for a while, so you can fix them before you publish.
- **Preview and Export** are in the profile menu, so you can see the report or save it without going through Publish.

*Excel*
- Every column with a fixed set of values has a **dropdown** with clark's own values, and a changed value takes its colour. An overdue date turns red.
- The **ID column is shaded** so you can see it is not for editing. Nothing in the workbook is locked, so you can still sort, filter and add rows.
- **Import:** a new row with no ID gets the next free ID, and the import plan says which. A changed ID is refused for that row, in words, and the other rows still apply.
- Your own brand's picture sits on the Cover sheet when you have one. The sheets are named RAID, Client messaging and Baseline. A workbook made by 0.1.0 with the old names still imports.

*Keeping your data safe*
- **Backups.** clark copies your data before every Undo, before every Excel import and once a day, into a folder beside your data (never in OneDrive). It keeps the newest 14. **Settings › Safety** lists them, newest first: look at one before you restore it. The steps to put one back from Terminal are written in plain words, and a test follows them.
- **Archive instead of delete.** clark keeps what it would have removed. Projects, work items, milestones, use cases and documents are archived, never deleted: an archived item leaves the lists, the counts and the published page, and Undo brings it back. A row you take out of an Excel workbook is archived on import, not removed. The Archive and Restore buttons on the page itself come in a later version.
- Closing a work item that was raised in the future now says so in plain words instead of failing.
- The checks on the published file are stricter, so nothing meant for the team can slip into it through a hidden web link or a trick in its styling.
- clark's start-up file starts and stops it more cleanly, and says in plain words what to do if something else is using what clark needs.

**Not yet:** the peek panel and Sneak peek, Word, ask clark, rich text formatting in the editor (the editor is in, but switched on in a later version), and downloads for Windows and Linux.

**Install (Mac):**
1. Download `clark-0.2.0-mac.zip` below and unzip it.
2. clark needs **Google Chrome** installed, and Python 3.9 or newer. To check your Mac is ready, open Terminal in the unzipped folder and type `python3 -m clark.doctor`. It tells you what to fix, if anything, and where to get it.
3. Double-click `clark.command`. `START-HERE.txt` in the folder explains the prompts your Mac may show the first time.

Keep clark's data folder outside OneDrive. If you already use 0.1.0 with real data, copy your data folder somewhere safe before you start 0.2.0.
